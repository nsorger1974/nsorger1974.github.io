Journey 01◆March 7–18, 2027◆35°10′N  5°16′W◆Tangier to Marrakech

Morocco

7–9 days, starting at the crossroads of Europe and Africa, Tangier, overlooking the Strait of Gibraltar, making our way to the breathtaking blue city of Chefchaouen in the Rif Mountains, winding through the labyrinth of streets in Fes dating back to the 8th century, and wrapping up with the wild street performers and delectable cuisine of Marrakech, all the while reveling in the colorful pottery, tiles, tanneries and lanterns of the vibrant medinas.

The inaugural Certain Latitude journey. We arrive in Tangier during the Eid al-Fitr holidays, when the medina is at its most festive, and delight in the aromatic wonders of street food chefs at dusk. Ten to fourteen of us, no more.

Six cities, north to south

Day by day

The whole itinerary

  • Evening nonstop from SFO to Paris-Charles de Gaulle, arriving the morning of Monday, March 8.

Hôtel Bachaumont 18 Rue Bachaumont, 2nd arrondissement

A restored 1900s hotel on a quiet market street, walking distance to the Marais and the Louvre. Alternative: Hôtel des Grands Boulevards.

  • Morning arrival at Charles de Gaulle; transfer into the city and settle in.
  • Afternoon: the Musée d’art et d’histoire du Judaïsme in the Marais, whose North African and Moroccan collections make a fitting overture to the days ahead.
  • Late afternoon: wander the Marais — Place des Vosges, the Rue des Rosiers, and the old lanes around the Village Saint-Paul.
  • Evening at leisure.
The group comes together this evening at Hôtel Bachaumont. From here on, everyone travels together.
La Tangerina 19 Riad Sultan, Kasbah, Tangier · Night 1

An intimate restored riad set into the kasbah walls, with terraces looking across the Strait of Gibraltar toward Spain. Alternative: Hôtel Nord-Pinus Tanger, a landmark property above the medina.

  • Morning flight from Charles de Gaulle to Casablanca — about 3 hours 15 minutes — arriving around midday.
  • Transfer to the station and the high-speed train to Tangier: 2 hours 10 minutes at up to 320 km/h (200 mph), first class, with the Atlantic plain streaming past the window.
  • Late afternoon arrival; transfer up to the kasbah and check in.
  • Evening: a first walk down through the medina to the Petit Socco, then mint tea at Café Hafa on the bluff above the water — a Tangier institution since 1921 — as the sun goes down over the strait.

Alhambra Ryad and Spa Chefchaouen Medina · Nights 2 & 3

A stunning riad with mountain and medina views, a celebrated rooftop terrace and attentive staff. In-house dining available. Alternative: Riad Sultan Chefchaouen.

  • Lunch in Tangier, then depart by private bus for Chefchaouen: about 2.5 hours through the green Rif foothills.
  • Late afternoon arrival: check in and set out into the famous blue-washed alleyways, with a rooftop sunset over the Rif Mountains.

Karawan Riad 21 Derb Ourbia Makhfiya, Fes el-Bali Medina · Nights 4, 5 & 6

A restored historic building in the heart of the medina.

  • Early departure by private bus for Fes — about 4 hours — arriving in time for lunch.

  • Visit the Ibn Danan Synagogue, one of the oldest surviving synagogues in North Africa, and the hillside Jewish cemetery above it.

A modern hotel in the Oasis district, a five-minute walk from the Museum of Moroccan Judaism. Superb breakfast and spacious rooms.

★ The Museum of Moroccan Judaism 81 Rue Chasseur Jules Gros, Oasis district · Mon–Fri, 10 AM–5 PM

The only Jewish museum in the Arab world, founded in 1997 in a 1948 orphanage. Torah scrolls, Berber Jewish costume, synagogue reconstructions, and the rare Megilat Hitler manuscript. Allow 1.5–2 hours.

Riad Dar Anika 112 Riad Zitoun Lakdim, Marrakech Medina · Nights 8 & 9

An exquisite traditional riad within the medina, walking distance to Jemaa el-Fna. Outstanding hospitality and refined Moroccan style. Alternative: Palais Sebban, a historic property recently renovated.

  • Unhurried breakfast at the hotel.
  • 10:00 AM: the Museum of Moroccan Judaism at opening — a five-minute walk from the hotel.
  • Light lunch in the Oasis district.
  • Mid-afternoon: the train from Casablanca to Marrakech — a comfortable 3-hour run across the Chaouia plains, first class, with departures every one to two hours.
  • Early evening: private transfer to the riad, then Jemaa el-Fna at dusk — storytellers, gnawa musicians, snake charmers, and the great open-air food stalls.

Getting there and home

Flights

Outbound · March 7–9

Out to Tangier

From San Francisco: SFO to Paris-Charles de Gaulle, evening nonstop on Sunday, March 7, arriving the morning of Monday, March 8 for a day and a night in the city.

From anywhere else: make your own way to Paris and join us there. Arrive at any point on Monday, March 8. We gather that evening at Hôtel Bachaumont.

Onward, Tuesday, March 9: Paris to Casablanca in the morning, about 3 hours 15 minutes, with several daily departures on Air France and Royal Air Maroc — then the high-speed train to Tangier in early afternoon, 2 hours 10 minutes in first class, departures every hour.

Home · Option A, March 18

Good to know

The practical part

Transport
Private bus for inter-city transfers, plus two first-class rail legs — the high-speed train north to Tangier on arrival, and the train to Marrakech at the end. No drive longer than four hours.
Group size
Ten to fourteen travelers, ages 40s–60s.
Included
All hotels, meals, domestic travel, guides, tips and entrance fees.
Not included
Travel insurance, which is required of every traveler — compare policies at Squaremouth — and international flights from your city of origin.
Time change
Morocco moves its clocks forward one hour the Sunday after Eid — likely March 14, mid-trip.
Language
French and Spanish are widely spoken in the north alongside Arabic and Darija; English is understood in tourist areas.
Currency
Moroccan dirham (MAD). ATMs are widely available; many restaurants prefer cash.
Dress
Shoulders and knees covered is respectful of local custom.
Tipping
10–15% is customary at restaurants; small tips for guides and drivers are appreciated.
Weather in March
Mild in the cities, cool in the mountains. Snow on the Middle Atlas peaks is common — bring layers and a warm outer shell.

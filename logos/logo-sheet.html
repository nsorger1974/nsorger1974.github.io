Certain Latitude · identity

Three directions

Same palette and typefaces as the website, so whichever you pick will already match. All three are vector files with the lettering converted to outlines, which means they will render identically everywhere and never depend on a font being installed.

A

The Parallel

Why it works

A globe reduced to the one line that matters, with the parallel running straight off the edge and carrying on. The brass diamond is the same marker used on the latitude scale on the website. It says travel immediately, at any size, to anyone.

What you give up

Most literal of the three, and the most conventional. If you ever want a mark that reads instantly as a travel company on a crowded Instagram grid, this is it.

B

The Khatem

Why it works

The eight-pointed star of Moroccan zellige — the same motif that runs east through Uzbek tilework, which is part of why those two destinations belong on one map. A star inside a star, with the brass marker at the centre.

What you give up

The most beautiful and the least obvious. It reads as ornament before it reads as travel, and it is tied to two of your three destinations rather than to the brand idea. It would embroider superbly.

C

The Meridian

Why it works

No icon at all. A single hairline parallel runs through the name and ends in brass at each edge — the line you are standing on, passing through. The name does the work.

What you give up

The most grown-up of the three and the closest in feel to the website. It needs horizontal room, so the stacked version below is the one for square spaces and profile pictures.

What’s in the folder

Every concept comes in a full-colour and a reversed (white-on-navy) version. SVG scales to any size without blurring — use it for the website, and hand the same file to a printer or an embroiderer.

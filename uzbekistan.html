Uzbekistan

April 2029. Explore the majestic Silk Road cities — Samarkand, Bukhara and Khiva — that first connected the cultures of East and West, with multiple faith communities and ethnicities living side by side in an early historical model of coexistence and exchange of ideas. Blue tiled gates five thousand miles east of Morocco provide an opening to the wonders of early travelers and their journeys of discovery.

Uzbekistan is still off-the-beaten-track, but ripe for exploring. High-speed trains connect historic Silk Road cities that brought music, spices, trade and cultural exchange from China through Central Asia en route to Europe, and Uzbekistan retains the most storied and best preserved cities along the route. In our interconnected 21st century world, what better time to explore cities in which those contacts and encounters were first made? We will venture along ancient Silk Road routes while admiring the pristine courtyards of old cities that match the color of the sky, and avoiding the crowds of more commonly-traversed cities of Eastern Europe.

Why here

Because the blue keeps going east

The cornflower blue of a Chefchaouen wall and the cobalt of a Samarkand dome are cousins. The tile-makers, the geometry, the eight-pointed star repeating across a wall until you stop counting — it is one long conversation stretching from Morocco to the Fergana Valley, and we would like to hear both ends of it.

Practically, it is one of the easiest places we could take a group of fourteen. High-speed trains connect the four cities we want. The old towns are compact and flat. And the hospitality is close to overwhelming.

Places we’re scouting

The shape of the trip

The great Silk Road cities are certain. What sits around them — the craft valley, the mountains, the desert night — is what we are still deciding.

41°18′N · Certain

Tashkent

Where we land. Soviet-era metro stations built like ballrooms, the Chorsu bazaar under its blue dome, and a first night to get our bearings.

39°39′N · Certain

Samarkand

The Registan at dusk, the tiled stairway of Shah-i-Zinda, and Ulugh Beg’s observatory — a sultan who measured the year to within a minute.

39°46′N · Certain

Bukhara

The most intact old town in Central Asia: Poi Kalyan, the Ark, and the trading domes where silk, hats and coins were each sold under their own roof.

41°22′N · Likely

Khiva

The walled Ichan Kala, small enough to walk end to end before breakfast, and best seen from the walls at sunrise when it is entirely empty.

40°28′N · Under discussion

The Fergana Valley

Silk woven by hand at Margilan and the blue-glaze ceramics of Rishtan — the makers behind the patterns we will have been staring at all week.

Optional

A night in the desert

Yurts near the Aydarkul lake for those who want it, and an extra night in Bukhara for those who would rather not. Nobody is made to sleep on the ground.
